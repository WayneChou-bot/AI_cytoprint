Honest reading (recomputed after fixing an extreme-value contamination bug): the three methods have almost identical biology signal (mAP raw 0.087 / sphered 0.087 / +Harmony 0.097; every pairwise paired-block Δ has a 95% CI that crosses zero, i.e. no significant difference). On the batch axis, Harmony clearly improves mixing (2.42× → 1.72×, toward 1) at no measurable biology cost; sphering alone is slightly worse.

Lesson: de-biasing must be judged on both axes at once — biology retention and batch removal — using a plate-block bootstrap and paired Δ CIs. This is a single-centre pilot where batch effects are small by construction, so most differences are not significant and should not be generalized. The same engine (jump_mvp.py) on multi-source JUMP, with source as the block, is where correction really earns its keep.

(For contrast: an earlier version reported "Harmony loses on both axes" — that was an artefact of contaminated data. After cleaning, the conclusion reversed, which is exactly why data quality and rigorous evaluation are both indispensable.)
